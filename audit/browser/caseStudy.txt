		87 text Proof of delivery and CMR scans arrive by email. Dispatchers match each one to an order in the TMS by hand, then attach it to the invoice. That is about 220 documents a day. Timed over three days, each took 4.1 minutes. In a sample of 300 invoices, 7.3% had the wrong attachment. Complaints about late invoicing were common.
		88 text Velho built the smallest version that could work. An inbox rule sends each scan to OCR, which looks for the order number and falls back to fuzzy matching on reference and plate. Above 90% confidence the document is attached automatically. Anything else goes into a queue sheet for a person. Version 1 does not write back to the TMS. It produces a draft attachment list and a CSV import, because the vendor API was a six-week wait. In week two, OCR on handwritten PODs turned out to be poor for about 12% of documents, so we added a 'needs human' queue.
		89 text The pilot started in week three with one dispatcher, Tiina. A second dispatcher, Joonas, was skeptical at first. After the pilot he asked for the same thing on export documents. By week four all three dispatchers were using it. A 15-minute weekly review of the queue's misses keeps it improving.
		90 text In the five-day pilot, 71% of documents were matched automatically, and 2 of 310 checked were wrong. Average handling time fell to 1.4 minutes per document, queue included. 		94 text 220 delivery documents a day. Each one matched to an order by hand. 4.1 minutes apiece.

That was a Finnish freight forwarder's reality before we timed it with their dispatchers.

We built the smallest thing that could work: OCR reads the scan, finds the order number and attaches it when it's confident. Anything unclear goes to a short queue for a human. No TMS changes in v1.

Pilot, one dispatcher, one week: 71% of documents matched automatically, 1.4 minutes per document including the queue, invoices out in 3.1 days instead of 5.2.

The skeptic on the team asked for the same on export documents.

Small sample, so we're still tuning.
